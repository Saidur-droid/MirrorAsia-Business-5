# Simile & Aaru Reference Model

## Simile — primary inspiration

Public/company-reported story used as category validation:

- founded in 2025,
- public launch in 2026,
- approximately $100M Series A,
- approximately $200M Series B five months later,
- around $2B valuation reported with the Series B,
- company reported 5x revenue growth in five months,
- company reported tens of millions of simulations,
- customers named publicly include CVS Health, Wealthfront, Deloitte and Gallup.

The important lesson is not the exact funding amount.

The important lesson is the business pattern:

1. persistent synthetic populations,
2. enterprise decision support,
3. repeated simulations,
4. product/research workflows,
5. high-value enterprise contracts,
6. potentially API/platform economics,
7. trust built through research pedigree and enterprise proof.

## Research-to-company path

Simile founder Joon Sung Park is associated with Stanford work on generative agents.

This matters because the company is not merely a prompt wrapper.

The category starts from a serious research question:

> Can simulated agents behave in ways useful enough to predict or explore human outcomes?

MirrorAsia should adopt the same research discipline.

## Aaru — secondary category validation

Aaru is another synthetic-research/population-simulation company.

Public reporting in late 2025 described:
- a large Series A,
- billion-dollar headline valuation mechanics,
- rapidly growing revenue,
- enterprise/consulting relationships including Accenture and EY.

Lesson:

> This category has more than one credible venture-backed company.

## What MirrorAsia should copy

- persistent populations,
- enterprise use cases,
- repeated simulations,
- research credibility,
- high-value workflows,
- API/platform direction,
- continuous evaluation.

## What MirrorAsia should NOT copy blindly

- US population assumptions,
- generic Western consumer profiles,
- exact product UI,
- exact pricing,
- claims about accuracy,
- funding expectations.

## MirrorAsia-specific extension

Our bet is:

> emerging-market populations are underrepresented in generic models, making local data and calibration disproportionately valuable.

The advantage must be earned with data, not marketing.
