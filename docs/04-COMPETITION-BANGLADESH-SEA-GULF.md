# Competition: Bangladesh, SEA & Gulf

## Bangladesh

### Open Asia Technologies / Nira
Source research describes OAT as:
- founded 2026,
- very small team,
- Nira in pre-launch/build stage,
- focus on AI-assisted Bangla consumer interviewing.

Overlap:
- local research,
- AI,
- consumer insight.

Difference:
- Nira appears oriented toward interviewing real consumers.
- MirrorAsia aims at persistent calibrated synthetic populations + simulations.

Treat them as a serious emerging competitor, not something to dismiss.

### Traditional research
Kantar Bangladesh and other agencies/panels already serve:
- quantitative research,
- qualitative research,
- product development,
- concept testing,
- creative testing,
- consumer panels.

They can be:
- competitors,
- data partners,
- customers,
- validation partners.

MirrorAsia should not claim surveys are obsolete.

## Southeast Asia

### TwinSim AI
Source research described TwinSim as a very early company positioning around synthetic personas across India/Gulf/SEA.

This indicates the category is emerging regionally.

### Other synthetic research tools
Expect global tools to enter quickly.

The barrier must therefore be:
**local panel + calibration + benchmarks + outcome history.**

## Competitive strategy

Do not compete on:
- persona count,
- prompt quality,
- synthetic respondent volume.

Compete on:
- benchmark accuracy,
- local data,
- category depth,
- freshness,
- human verification,
- enterprise workflow,
- explainability.

## Bangladesh competition playbook

1. move quickly on a real respondent panel,
2. lock one FMCG benchmark suite,
3. win 2–3 design partners,
4. publish calibration methodology,
5. partner with agencies instead of fighting all of them,
6. build data moat before flashy UI.

## Global competition playbook

Against Simile/Aaru/global platforms:

Do not claim better general AI.

Claim and prove:
- better Bangladesh calibration,
- better Bangla/Banglish nuance,
- better local pricing/cultural context,
- higher confidence on specific segments,
- transparent benchmark error.

## Important rule

If a global competitor becomes better on Bangladesh, our differentiation must still survive through proprietary data, refresh cycles, customer-specific outcome history and category benchmarks.
