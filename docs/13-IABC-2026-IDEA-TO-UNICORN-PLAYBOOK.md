## 2. Current official preliminary submission requirements

The official Submission Rules v1.1 require:

1. **1-page structured project summary**
2. **3-minute concept or prototype demo video**

The submission must clearly include:

- clear problem definition,
- target users,
- AI-native solution,
- basic system flow,
- initial demo, prototype, mock flow, or system walkthrough,
- AI approach: models, tools, RAG, data, or workflow,
- Bangla or localization consideration,
- potential impact and measurable outcomes,
- next steps.

The rules explicitly say teams must show **real system thinking, not only an idea or visual concept**.

### Recommended 180-second video structure from the Participant Guide

- **0:00–0:30 — Problem**
- **0:30–1:00 — Solution**
- **1:00–2:00 — Demo / system flow**
- **2:00–2:30 — AI approach**
- **2:30–3:00 — Impact + next step**

### MirrorAsia video storyline

**Problem:** Consumer concept research is too slow and expensive to repeat across many prices, packs, messages, and segments.

**Solution:** MirrorAsia simulates many concepts against a calibrated local population, exposes uncertainty, and sends important cases to real-human validation.

**Demo:**  
1. Upload two FMCG package concepts.  
2. Select Bangladesh → Dhaka → target segment.  
3. Run simulated population.  
4. Show segment preference and reasons.  
5. Show Confidence Lab / nearest benchmark / uncertainty.  
6. Click Human Check.  
7. Show how new real-human results would update calibration.

**AI approach:** Explain the population schema, simulation agents, retrieval/context layer, aggregation, benchmark comparison, calibration loop, confidence engine, and population versioning.

**Impact:** Faster concept screening, more variants tested, lower research waste, and better prioritization before expensive field research.

## 3. Official judging framework — target this version

The current official Judging Rules v1.1 and event page use a **100-point framework**:

| Criterion | Weight | What MirrorAsia must prove |
|---|---:|---|
| Innovation | 20 | Non-obvious AI application; not a generic persona generator |
| Technical Execution | 20 | Real architecture, real AI integration, reliability, explainability |
| Business Model + Global Readiness | 20 | Monetization, adoption, cross-border viability, demand evidence |
| Real-World Impact + Ethical AI Compliance | 20 | Measurable benefit, responsible data use, bias/privacy safeguards |
| Scalability + NRB Collaboration | 10 | Cloud/modular scaling, international collaboration, deployment pathway |
| Presentation | 10 | Clear, compelling, data-driven, investor-ready demonstration |

### Score-band target

The event page defines the top bands roughly as:

- **Innovation 16–20:** breakthrough or globally competitive innovation
- **Technical 16–20:** production-grade engineering maturity
- **Business 16–20:** scalable, globally adaptable economic model with strong potential
- **Impact 16–20:** high social/economic return with documented ethical safeguards
- **Scalability 8–10:** clear deployment pathway + strong global collaboration readiness
- **Presentation 8–10:** compelling, data-driven, investor-ready

### Important rubric conflict

The Participant Guide Section 11 currently also shows a different presentation of the rubric: **five components × 20 points**.

Because the **official Judging Rules v1.1 and live event page** show the six-part 20/20/20/20/10/10 rubric, MirrorAsia should optimize for the official live rubric unless CloudCamp publishes a newer controlling version.

## 4. How MirrorAsia should target the top band in each criterion

### A. Innovation — 20

Do **not** pitch:

> "We make AI personas."

Pitch:

> "MirrorAsia is a human-calibrated population intelligence system that measures where synthetic predictions match or fail against real people, then recalibrates the population over time."

Must demonstrate:

- persistent population model, not one-off personas,
- Bangladesh-specific calibration,
- Bangla/Banglish and local economic/cultural variables,
- benchmark history,
- explicit uncertainty,
- **MirrorScore** scoped by market/category/segment/time,
- population versioning,
- human validation feedback loop.

Strongest innovation claim:

> **The product does not hide synthetic uncertainty; it turns prediction error into a measurable product layer.**

Avoid claiming that synthetic consumers replace real people.

---

### B. Technical Execution — 20

A slide deck is not enough.

Minimum working architecture:

```text
Scenario / Product / Price / Creative
        ↓
Input normalizer + metadata
        ↓
Population selector
        ↓
Population schema + local context
        ↓
Simulation / agent execution layer
        ↓
Structured response aggregation
        ↓
Segment analysis
        ↓
Benchmark retrieval
        ↓
Calibration + confidence engine
        ↓
MirrorScore / uncertainty output
        ↓
Human Check → new evidence → population update
```

For the top band, the prototype should show:

- functioning frontend,
- functioning backend/API,
- model calls or simulation engine,
- structured outputs rather than free-form chat only,
- retrieval/context grounding where useful,
- repeatable runs,
- benchmark/evaluation logic,
- source/provenance tracking,
- error handling,
- privacy controls,
- versioned population/configuration,
- deployable cloud architecture,
- test/evaluation suite.

### Minimum technical proof before submission

- one end-to-end FMCG use case,
- at least two segments,
- at least two concepts or price points,
- reproducible result schema,
- Confidence Lab output,
- MirrorScore or a clearly labeled benchmark prototype,
- documented AI stack,
- no fake performance claims.

### C. Business Model + Global Readiness — 20

Initial buyer:

- FMCG innovation team,

Initial wedge:

> **FMCG concept, price, packaging, and message screening**

Global expansion story:

- agencies can access populations by workspace/API.

### D. Real-World Impact + Ethical AI Compliance — 20

MirrorAsia's biggest risk is **wrong prediction**.

Therefore ethics must be part of the architecture.

Required safeguards:

- explicit consent for respondent data,
- anonymization/pseudonymization,
- data minimization,
- tenant isolation,
- opt-out/deletion process,
- no sale of raw identities,
- source/provenance documentation,
- bias checks by segment,
- confidence downgrade when evidence is weak,
- human validation for weak/high-stakes cases,
- population versioning and drift monitoring,
- no political persuasion,
- no credit/insurance decisioning,
- no individual health diagnosis,
- no employment screening,
- no sensitive individual manipulation.

Impact KPIs to show:

- time to first concept result,
- number of variants tested,
- rank agreement vs. human benchmark,
- preference error,
- overconfidence rate,
- benchmark freshness,
- cost per simulation,
- human-check conversion.

If real benchmark data is not ready, label the evaluation as **prototype / benchmark plan**. Never fabricate accuracy.

## 9. MirrorAsia's biggest competition risks

### Risk 1 — "This is just ChatGPT personas"
Defense:
- calibrated population distributions,
- benchmark error,
- real-human refresh,
- longitudinal population versions,
- confidence tiers,
- MirrorScore.

### Risk 2 — "How do you know the prediction is right?"
Defense:
- never claim guaranteed correctness,
- show benchmark methodology,
- show scoped evaluation,
- show confidence downgrade,
- show Human Check,
- show how calibration changes after evidence arrives.

### Risk 3 — "Simile/Aaru already exist"
Defense:
- local emerging-market population data,
- Bangladesh-specific calibration,
- Bangla/Banglish behavior,
- local purchasing power and household variables,
- transparent benchmark error,
- regional country-pack expansion.

### Risk 4 — "No real product, only a business plan"
Defense:
- ship one complete vertical slice before submission,
- deploy it publicly or privately for judging,
- record a real workflow in the 3-minute video,
- document architecture and evaluation.

## Final operating rule for MirrorAsia

For this competition, prioritize:

> **working system + measured trust + honest uncertainty + business clarity + regional scalability**

over:

> **more features + bigger claims + prettier AI-generated personas**

The strongest competition version of MirrorAsia is not the one that simulates the most people.

It is the one that can best prove:

> **when its simulations should be trusted, when they should not be trusted, and how real-human evidence continuously makes the system better.**
